# Changelog

What's new in each version of CIMA Box. This file is built into the app (Settings > Version
history, and the "What's new" window after an update) and becomes each GitHub release's notes.

To release a version, add a section for it at the top: `## 2.7.0`, then one line per change
starting with `- `. Write them for the people using the app.

## 3.1.9
- New: a beta access key in Settings > Updates, for people invited to try test versions before everyone gets them. Without a key, nothing changes.

## 3.1.8
- New: a Usage page (in the left side, under Extra features; clicking the credits box at the bottom left opens it too). Pick a service along the top (ElevenLabs, Claude, Gemini, OpenAI, DeepL, Microsoft Azure, or the model on your PC) and a time (this session, today, 7 days, 30 days), and it shows what that service was used for: My voice and Text, Listen's finished captions, Listen while they talk, "What my translated line means", Hear them in my ear, Changer and Re-speak. ElevenLabs shows the credits left, when they renew, the minutes of sound and letters each part used, and your last 7 days of credits from your own ElevenLabs account. Claude, Gemini and OpenAI show requests, tokens, how much came from their cache, and what that comes to at list price, with the last requests of the session (only counts and times are kept, never what anyone said). DeepL shows this month's letters from your account; Microsoft doesn't tell apps, so CIMA Box counts its letters itself and says so. The days are kept on this PC (usage-history.json, next to the settings, two months), and nothing on the page is sent anywhere.
- New, Listen: captions can use a different translation service from My voice and Text. On the Listen page, "Translation service" now starts with "Same as My voice and Text" (what it always was, and still the default), followed by every service. Pick another one and only captions (and screen translation) use it: for example Gemini or the free model on your PC for what others say, and Claude for your own voice. Learning mode stays with the Text page's service. The model on your PC is loaded when either one uses it, and the usage box at the bottom left shows both services.
- Cheaper, Listen with "Captions while they talk" and an AI translator: each finished sentence was sent to the AI as soon as it appeared, but the words heard while someone is still talking keep being corrected, and every corrected version of a sentence was sent again. A sentence is now sent once its words have stayed the same for half a second (or at once when they stop talking). What you see is the same; a sentence's translation can show up to half a second later while they're still talking. Tried with a stand-in for the speech service and the AI: one phrase that went through twelve corrections sent 4 requests instead of 6. Also, a short sentence said in your own language could look like another one on its own ("No way." looks Spanish, "Hold on." French) and was sent to be translated into the language it was already in; when the rest of what they're saying is clearly in your language, it now isn't.
- Cheaper, My voice with Claude: most of Claude's instructions are the same on every line, so Claude keeps them for a few minutes and charges a tenth for them after the first time. One rule depended on the line (with Carry my emotion, whether it has directions like [laughs]), and it sat in the middle, so each time a line with directions followed one without, the whole thing was paid in full again. That rule now comes after the part Claude keeps. Tried on six lines with Claude: the same translations, and the kept part was paid for once instead of twice.
- Fixed, Listen with "They speak" set to a language (and "Only this language" off): every line was taken to be in that language, so with "They speak: Arabic" and people speaking English, each English line was sent to be "translated" into English, and "Skip speech in my language" never left it out. A line that is clearly in your own language is now treated as that: not translated, and left out with "Skip speech in my language" on. With a language written in its own letters (Arabic, Russian, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and others), a line written only in Latin letters that looks like your language counts too, so short replies like "Sorry." are caught. Tried with the real Listen code and a stand-in for the speech service: three English lines with "They speak: Arabic" went from three translation requests to none, and an Arabic line was translated as before. One case is left: told to write Arabic, the speech service sometimes writes English speech down in Arabic letters, and that can't be told apart from Arabic. "Only this language" avoids it, because the service is then not told the language.
- New, the log: every ten minutes (when something changed) and when CIMA Box closes, a line with how many requests each AI service was sent in this session, what for (My voice, Listen captions, Listen sentences while they talk, other), how many tokens went in and out, how many came from the service's cache (Claude, Gemini and OpenAI each keep the repeated start of the instructions), and about what that cost. Your bill on the service's own site can be checked against it.

## 3.1.7
- Heads up: this version changes how voices are told apart and how laughs and coughs are heard. The changes were worked out from the log of one long evening in VRChat and haven't been tried in many rooms yet. If voices get mixed up more than before, or the voice laughs when you didn't, send the log (Settings > About > Open log file).
- New, Listen: "Only this language". Pick a language under "They speak" and the switch appears below it. With it on, only speech in that language gets a caption, and everything else is left out: in a room where one group speaks Arabic and another Chinese, you read only the one you picked. Before, "They speak" only told the speech service which language to write the words down in, so other languages were still captioned, as nonsense. A very short reply can't always be told apart; with "Same voice, same color" on, it goes by who said it, and from a voice nobody knows yet it is shown.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion: a cough or a laugh on its own failed with v4 Turbo ("Input at position 0 has empty text") and nothing was said. When a voice model refuses a sound on its own, the sound now goes with a short word after it ("[coughs] Ahem."), and CIMA Box remembers that for the rest of the session.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion with live transcription: laughs and coughs were almost never heard. The sound recogniser on your PC was given whole seconds of you talking, with the laugh somewhere under it. It now also listens to the gaps between your words on their own (live transcription says when each word was said), which is where a laugh or a cough is.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion with an AI translator: when you laughed and it was written down as "haha", the AI wrote [laughs] in the translation, and CIMA Box took that for a mistake and removed every direction from the line. A laugh the AI writes that way is now kept, with everything else in place.
- Fixed, "Same voice, same color" with voice fingerprints: half of all lines stayed unnamed in a busy room. Three causes. A line counted as "too little voice" by how much clear pitch it had, which game audio has little of, even with a good fingerprint in hand; it now goes by how much speech the fingerprint was made from. A new person who sounded a bit like someone already known was neither matched nor remembered, every time they spoke; two such lines that are clearly the same voice now make a new voice. And one person who had ended up with two numbers kept tying with themselves and got no name; when the same two numbers keep tying and are alike enough, they become one.
- New, Listen: "Skip sounds that aren't a voice" (on). Listen cuts the sound into phrases by loudness alone, so a door, footsteps, a fan or a game's music was a phrase too: sent to the speech service, paid for, and in the queue ahead of the people talking. A small voice-detection model on your PC now checks each phrase first, in a few hundredths of a second, and a phrase with no voice in it goes no further. It is a small download (about 10 MB) that Listen gets by itself the first time. Tried on sample recordings: voices were always let through (quiet ones, and ones under music or a fan as loud as the voice), and a fan, hiss, knocks and clicks were always stopped. Music is stopped only sometimes. It is off while "Sound labels" is on, since those want the sounds.
- New, Listen: "Clean noisy phrases (trial)" (off). A hiss, a fan or a hum is taken out from under a voice before the words are written down, for phrases that are noisy to begin with. It is a trial because it could not be tried against the real speech service without spending credits: turn it on in a noisy room and see whether the words get better or worse. It does not touch the words shown while someone is still talking, and it is never used for telling voices apart: tried on sample recordings, cleaned voices were named right more often but named WRONG three times as often, because cleaning makes different people sound more alike.
- Better, Listen: the rumble under a voice (a boomy mic, a fan, a game's bass) is taken out before the words are written down and the voice is recognised, and a quiet phrase (someone far away, a low mic) is brought up to an ordinary level first. Loud phrases are left as they are.
- Better, AI translation: what you said now reaches the AI inside a tag of its own, and the AI is told that everything in it is speech to say, never a message to it. A line like "what's your name?" or "are you an AI?" could be taken as a question to the AI itself.
- Better, AI translation: slang is kept to words people everywhere in that language know, spelled the usual way, and when the AI isn't sure of a slang word it uses the ordinary one. "Very casual" told it slang was expected wherever a slang word exists, and a smaller model would reach for regional or made-up words. It is also told to write no word in capitals unless you said a name or an abbreviation yourself (the voice reads capitals out letter by letter).
- New, AI translation: a free look at every AI line before it is said. It uses no tokens and no other service, and adds no wait. It can't tell whether a line means what you said; it catches what shows in the letters alone: a word in capitals that nobody said, a line for Japanese, Russian, Arabic and the like with none of their writing in it, and a long line answered with a few letters. Such a line goes to DeepL or Microsoft Translator if you have a key for one (that line then counts against their characters), and the strip at the bottom says why. Without a key the AI's line is said as before.
- Better, a model on your PC (Local): it gets a much shorter set of instructions for Casual and Very casual, with this language's own informal forms, and uses a slang word only when it is sure of it. For Spanish, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Russian and Indonesian it also gets three example lines in that language (when no dialect is picked). These examples were written for this version and have not been read by native speakers yet: if Local sounds worse in your language than before, say so.
- Fixed, "I am" and "Talking to": the gender you set was followed in a full sentence and dropped in a short reply. "I'm good" said by a woman came out in the masculine form (Gulf Arabic "zein" where it should be "zeina"), and with "Talking to: a girl", "are you tired?" came out masculine. The AI now gets the rule with this language's own forms in front of it, is told that a one-word reply follows it too, and checks it once more before answering. Tried on a model on this PC with Arabic, Kuwaiti Arabic, Spanish, Russian and Portuguese: the short lines that were wrong came out right, and the ones that were right stayed right. Not tried with Claude, Gemini or OpenAI, which get the same rule.
- Fixed: "Short lines skip the AI" sent a short line to DeepL or Microsoft even when "I am" is set to a woman or nonbinary, or "Talking to" to a girl. Those services word a line the same way for everyone, so the setting was lost on exactly the short replies. Such a line now goes to the AI.
- Better: on My voice, under "I am", it now says so when the translation service you picked doesn't follow "I am" and "Talking to" at all (DeepL, Microsoft and Free don't; only an AI translator does).
- Faster, a model on your PC: it keeps its instructions in memory and reads only what is new, but three things in the middle of them changed from line to line (the recent conversation, whether the line carries directions from Carry my emotion, and one of its strict rules), and everything after the change was read again each time: about 400 tokens on every line said while others were talking. Those parts are now at the very end, so about 90 are read again. On a graphics card that brought the reading for such a line from about 216 to about 110 ms.
- Faster, the first line with a model on your PC: the instructions are read while you are still talking (the first time, and again after you change the language, the dialect, the speech level or "I am"), not when the line arrives. On the processor that took the first line from 3.0 to 1.8 seconds.
- Fixed, a model on your PC: when the graphics card drops the model ("device lost": the driver resets, or the card's memory runs out under a game), the engine keeps running and answers nothing. CIMA Box only noticed minutes later, when the engine finally exited, and every line in between went to a backup or was lost. It now sees it within two seconds and starts the engine again; if it happens twice in ten minutes, the model runs on the processor until CIMA Box is started again, and says so.
- Fixed, a model on your PC: the engine's log was overwritten each time it started, so after a crash there was nothing left to say why. The log of the run before is now kept as engine-last.log (next to engine.log, in the local-models folder), and the last thing the engine wrote goes into the app's log.
- Fixed, a model on your PC: "is the graphics card full?" went by what the graphics driver says is free, and on some cards the driver says nearly all of it whatever a game is using (an AMD card reported 19.6 GB free of 20 with 13.7 GB in use). It now goes by what Windows counts, the same numbers as Task Manager. What the model itself took on the card is logged when it is ready, and used for that check the next time it starts.
- New, for anyone changing the AI instructions: a translation quality test in tools/quality. It runs 59 lines (common languages, "I am" and "Talking to", dialects, slang, gaming and VRChat talk, lines that must be said and not answered, Carry my emotion, Listen) through the app's own translation code against a model on your PC, at no cost, checks what a program can check, and writes every line and its translation to a report for a person to read. With these instructions 58 of the 59 pass; the one that doesn't writes "Discord" in Russian letters.
- New, a model on your PC: when the graphics card is nearly full (a game is using it), as much of the model as fits goes on the card and the rest runs on the processor. Before, it was all or nothing, and "full" went by a number that on some cards never moves. Measured on one card with a 4 GB model: all of it on the card writes 114 tokens a second, half of it 26, none of it 12. A gigabyte and a half of the card is always left free for the game. The app says when only part of the model fitted; it stays that way until the model is loaded again (pick another model and back, or start CIMA Box again).
- Faster, a model on your PC: the engine now keeps everything it has read in memory, not only the last 512 tokens of some layers. On a graphics card that made a line said while others were talking about a quarter quicker (244 to 187 ms) and Listen's captions a third quicker (290 to 190 ms), for 250 MB more graphics memory. Four other engine settings were tried and made no difference, so they were left alone. An engine that doesn't know the newer options is started the way it was before.
- Tested, a model on your PC: on one PC (an AMD card with 20 GB) with nothing else using the card: a normal start, a card pretended nearly full and full, an engine that refuses the newer options, and a lost graphics device. It could not be tested with a game running on the card: a second model next to a game ran many times slower and then lost the card, so that test was stopped.
- Better, a model on your PC: it often left out the tags its answer is meant to come in, which the app relies on to drop anything it adds around the line. They are now one of its strict rules.
- New: "Join our Discord", at the bottom of the left side on every page and in Settings > About. It opens CIMA Box's Discord server in your browser: questions, tips and news about updates, with other people who use CIMA Box.
- Better: the on/off switch at the top of My voice, Text, Listen and VR is larger and sits in a button of its own with the state next to it ("Off", "Listening"). The whole button can be clicked, and it lights up in your color when the page is on. The small switch in the corner was easy to miss, and a page left off looked like one that was on.
- The log says more, for Report a problem: how sure the sound recogniser was of the nearest laugh or cough when it didn't count one (it used to name "Speech synthesizer" almost every time, which said nothing), how many gaps between words it listened to, which language Listen is set to, and how many lines "Only this language" left out.

## 3.1.6
- Heads up, experimental: the new Carry my emotion work in this version is still being tuned and is not stable yet. That covers hearing laughs, coughs and other sounds, the sound recogniser that runs on your PC, placing each delivery and sound inside a translated line, and telling fast talking from excited. How well it works depends on your microphone, your voice, how fast you talk, the voice you picked and which translator does the line, so results will differ from one person and one PC to the next. Sounds in the middle of fast, continuous speech are the most likely to be missed. The rest of CIMA Box works as before. If it gets in your way, turn Carry my emotion off (My voice). If you want to help tune it, send your log with Report a problem.
- New, Carry my emotion with Translate: everything now lands where it happened. Before, a translated line got one delivery for the whole sentence and every sound was moved to the end, so whispering, then coughing, then talking fast came out as just "quickly". Now, with an AI translator (Claude, Gemini, OpenAI or a local model), the line is sent with each direction in place, and the AI carries each one over to the matching words: the whisper stays on what you whispered, the cough stays where you coughed. Stressed words carry over too: the word you leaned on is stressed in the translation, in languages written with capital letters. CIMA Box checks that every direction came back unchanged; if one didn't, or a backup translator (DeepL, Microsoft, Free) did the line, that line gets one delivery as before. Lines with nothing to carry are translated exactly as before.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion: laughs, coughs and sighs were lost whenever live transcription was on, because live transcription hears words only. CIMA Box now recognises them on your own PC: the parts of a phrase that aren't words go to a small sound-recognition model, which says what it heard, and the sound is put among your words before the voice speaks. Nothing extra is uploaded, it costs no credits, and it adds no wait you would notice. There is nothing to switch on: the recogniser (about 40 MB) downloads by itself the first time Carry my emotion is on with live transcription and a v3 or v4 voice. With live transcription off, sounds were already heard and nothing changes.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion: talking fast was often read as "excited" or "flatly". Fast speech runs a little higher and more even, which looked like both. A part said clearly faster than your usual pace is now "quickly" unless it is also livelier and louder, and "flatly" is only for a monotone at your usual pace or slower. It also takes less to count as fast (a quarter above your usual pace; less on Strong), and talking fast for a while no longer turns into your "usual" pace.
- Fixed, Carry my emotion: a laugh or a cough in a sentence could make the voice say it "excited" or shouted, because both are loud and high. A part with a laugh or cough in it is no longer read that way.
- New, Carry my emotion: a laugh or a cough on its own, with no words around it, is now made by the voice too. Before, a phrase with no words was dropped. Other wordless noise (a bump, a breath) is still left out.
- New, Carry my emotion: laughing while you talk is picked up as well, when it's clear, and goes at the start of the line.
- Easier to find: Carry my emotion is now on the My voice page itself, no longer folded away under "More voice settings".
- Easier to find: the switches that make translations faster are together under one "Faster translations" heading. The two that need live transcription have a "Turn on Live transcription" button right there when it's off.
- New, Carry my emotion: more sounds carry over: coughs, yawns, sniffs, sneezes, groans, grunts, snorts, hiccups, gulps, scoffs, whimpers, screams, moans, humming, wheezing and heavy breaths in or out. They were heard before but left out. How well the voice performs each one depends on the voice.
- The log now has a "Sounds:" line for each phrase, saying which sounds were heard and what became of each.
- CIMA Box now has a license (the LICENSE file on GitHub): the code is free to use and build on for anything noncommercial. Selling it, or using it in a paid product, needs permission.

## 3.1.5
- Fixed, local models: the first line after a model loaded could take 10 seconds or more and end up with a backup translator. The first answer after loading is always the slow one (the graphics card sets itself up on first use), and that used to be your first real line. The model now gives one throwaway answer while it still says "Loading the model", so your first line is as quick as the ones after it. A line said during that moment goes straight to the backup, without waiting.
- New, for NVIDIA cards: "Use NVIDIA's own engine" (Local models, under "Use the graphics card"; off by default, and only shown on PCs with an NVIDIA card). Local models then run on the engine NVIDIA makes for its own cards, which is usually faster than the standard one. The first time, it downloads about 0.6 GB more. If it can't be downloaded, won't start with your driver or can't load the model, CIMA Box goes back to the standard engine by itself and says so under the model, so switching it on can't leave you without a local model. Switching it off deletes its files again. AMD and Intel cards keep the standard engine, which is the right one for them.
- Faster local models on PCs with two graphics devices (NVIDIA or AMD card, plus the small chip built into the processor). The engine could put part of the model on the built-in chip, which made every line slow. CIMA Box now asks the engine what it sees and tells it to use the real card only. PCs with one card are unchanged.
- New: when a game is already using most of the graphics card's memory, the model spills into ordinary memory and gets slow. CIMA Box now checks how much of the card is free before loading and says so under the model ("The graphics card's memory is almost full..."), so you know to pick a lighter model or close something.
- The log now says which graphics card a local model runs on and how long its first answer took ("Gemma 4 E4B Uncensored is ready on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090. Its first answer took 2.1 s."), and how much of the card's memory was free, so a PC where it is slow can be told apart at a glance.

## 3.1.4
- Changed, local models: the three Gemma 4 models are now uncensored builds. They translate swearing, insults between friends and crude lines as they were said, where the stock Gemma softened or declined them.
  - Light (Gemma 4 E2B Uncensored, 3.5 GB download), Balanced (Gemma 4 E4B Uncensored, 5 GB) and Best (Gemma 4 12B Uncensored, 7.4 GB). They need a little more memory than the old ones: about 5, 7 and 11 GB.
  - If you downloaded one of the old Gemma models, nothing is deleted: it still shows in the Model list and keeps working, with a note that the new ones replace it. Delete it there when you have switched. The old ones are no longer offered for download.
  - One click cleans up: while an old model is still on your PC, the Local models section says how much space the old ones take and has a "Delete the older models" button. Download one of the new ones first, then press it.
  - Qwen 3.8 27B Uncensored is unchanged.
- Fixed: "Which means" sometimes came back in the wrong language (an English line explained in French). With the spoken language on automatic and no language reported for the line, it guessed from a few words, and "what's going on" looked French. It now uses the language you picked or the one that was heard, then the one language under "Languages I speak", a guess only when the words make it clear, and otherwise the language you read CIMA Box in.
- Better: the guess of a line's language from its words knows far more everyday English and French, so short English lines are no longer taken for French or Spanish as easily (Free translation and Listen use it too).

The window gets a little taller for it; make it taller still to see more. With Listen off, the mini window says so, with a button to turn it on. ## 3.1.2
- Faster captions while they talk: their voice is now streamed to ElevenLabs as they speak (the way your own voice already is), so their words show within a moment instead of every second and a half, and nothing is uploaded over and over.
- New: each sentence is translated as soon as it's finished, while they're still talking, so you read the first sentence while they say the next. When they stop, the whole caption is translated at once from the words that are already there; the regular pass (who said what, voices, sounds) then replaces it. Needs "Captions while they talk" and an AI translation service on the Listen page. The switch is "Translate each sentence right away" (Features).
- New, for crowded worlds: "In a crowd, caption" on the Listen page.
  - Nearby: only the loudest voices, which in VRChat are the people closest to you. The level a caption opens at follows the loudest voice of the last few seconds, so chatter further away gets no captions.
  - In front: only the voice you're facing. Sound from the sides is turned down before it's written down, and when nobody in front is talking, nothing opens a caption. It can't tell in front from behind, and needs stereo sound (an application or your speakers).
  - Everyone: as before.
- New (optional, off by default): "Say each sentence as soon as it's finished". When you say several sentences without a pause, the first one is translated and spoken while you're still on the next, instead of everything waiting for you to stop. The words after it wait for the rest of their own sentence, so nothing is spoken half finished. Needs Live transcription; short sentences ("Yeah.") stay with what follows.
- If the live connection isn't available, captions while they talk work the way they did before.
- Faster and cheaper captions when speakers aren't told apart: with "Captions while they talk" on and "Tell speakers apart" and sound labels off, the words that came over the live connection are the caption, and the audio isn't sent a second time.
- New, for busy rooms: voice fingerprints. "Same voice, same color" (Extra features, Caption options) has a Download button (about 45 MB, one click, nothing to install by hand). With it, voices are recognized by a speaker-recognition model that runs on your PC, instead of by pitch and tone alone, so up to 12 people keep their own number and color (6 before), and voices that sound alike are no longer mixed up as easily. It adds no delay: the fingerprint is worked out while the translation is on its way. Nothing extra is sent anywhere and it costs nothing per caption. Without the download everything works as before; you can delete it again from the same place. A line too short for a fingerprint (under about a second) is only named when it clearly continues whoever just spoke; otherwise it stays unnamed. When you change worlds and every number is taken, new people take over the numbers of whoever has been quiet the longest (at least a minute), so newcomers are never left without one.
- Faster, steadier AI translation. Four switches under the translation service (My voice and Listen), above Time limit:
  - New, on by default: "Read the AI's answer as it arrives". An answer that has begun by the time limit gets a moment more (about a second) to finish, instead of being thrown away for DeepL's plain version. More lines keep your dialect and speech level, and fewer are paid for twice. A service that is stuck is still dropped at the limit. The words are the same as before: nothing is used until the whole answer is in and checked, half an answer is never used, and a model whose answers keep arriving broken is asked the old way for the rest of the session. Works with Claude, Gemini, OpenAI and local models.
  - New, off by default: "Faster service tier (costs more)", for Gemini and OpenAI. Their priority tier answers sooner at a higher price per token (up to double; Gemini only for paid projects). If your account or model doesn't have it, CIMA Box carries on without it. Claude has no such option for its fast model.
  - New, off by default: "Remember repeated lines". A short line this AI already translated with the same language, dialect, speech level and speaker is answered at once and costs nothing. It comes out the same way every time, whatever the conversation around it, which is why it's your choice. Forgotten when CIMA Box closes.
  - New, off by default: "Short lines skip the AI" (needs a DeepL or Microsoft key). Lines of one to three words go straight to DeepL or Microsoft: much faster, but in the standard language, without your dialect or speech level.
- Better: the AI's instructions hold your settings more firmly where they meet each other. Nothing was removed from them.
  - Polite now names your language's own polite forms (usted, vous, Sie, 해요체, です/ます...), says that a casual original doesn't make the line casual, and checks the speech level once more before answering, the way Casual already did.
  - "Sound like" only shapes word choice. It can no longer quietly override the dialect, the speech level or the gender you set ("from Madrid" with the dialect set to Argentina: Argentina wins).
  - With your gender not set, the AI avoids gendered words about you where it can, and otherwise uses the same form in every line instead of switching.
  - When settings pull in different directions, the order is spelled out: what you mean first, then the dialect and the speech level together, then everything else.
  - "Match how they talk to me" can make a line looser, never add swearing or anything you didn't say.
  - Local models with "My own" style now get the same closing rules as the other styles (language or dialect, nothing added, one answer, punctuation kept).
- Better, voice fingerprints: two people no longer end up sharing a number.
  - A match has to stand clear of the next most similar voice. When a line could be either of two people, it's left unnamed instead of guessed.
  - A voice only learns from lines that are clearly its own, so a similar-sounding stranger can't blend into it.
  - Two voices are only merged into one number when they are very alike (this is where a second person used to be folded into Voice 1).
  - When two people talk over each other and are written down as one line, CIMA Box notices (the two halves of the line have different fingerprints) and leaves it unnamed.

- OpenAI is now a translation choice next to Claude and Gemini (GPT-6 Luna: quick and cheap, and you can try it on OpenAI's free plan). The setup and Settings have a step-by-step guide with pictures, and Check key tells you if the key works or the account needs credit.
- Casual and Very casual really are casual now. They no longer translate your sentence word for word: they say what you mean the way you'd say it to a friend (or your closest friend), however neatly you said it. Polite forms are ruled out, slang is expected on Very casual, each common language gets told what casual speech looks like in it, and polite people around you no longer pull you back to formal. DeepL, as your service or as the backup, now also uses informal words (du, tu, tú...) on the casual styles and polite ones on Polite, in the languages where it can.
- New "My own" style: describe how you want to sound in your own words, like "laid back and a bit sarcastic, like with my best friend".
- Fewer lines falling back to DeepL when you use a dialect: dialect lines get a little more time, a line the AI answers wrongly or refuses is asked again once, and a moment of "too busy" is retried instead of giving up. Gemini no longer silently drops lines with swearing.
- Arabic, Persian, Urdu and Hebrew now display properly everywhere: letters join up and lines read right to left, in the app, the conversation, captions and the VR panel.
- CIMA Box in Arabic and Indonesian: pick them in the language step or in Settings > App language.
- A short, one-time note about costs after the setup (and once after this update): CIMA Box runs on your own keys, so keep an eye on what you leave switched on and set a spending limit. Read it again any time in Settings > About.
- Service status now shows DeepL and Gemini in the app too, instead of sending you to their websites.
- A calmer look: softer colors, fewer outlines and a little more room between things.
- New Features page (the tab next to VR). Everything below is there, sorted into Talking, Hearing and reading, and Handy. Each one is off until you turn it on.
- Screen translation: translate text in a game or app, like a chat box, signs or menus. Pick the window, take a snapshot, drag over the text in the preview and press Translate; the result shows with your captions and in the conversation. "Watch this area" keeps translating new lines in a chat box. In a game, a hotkey or the button in the SteamVR menu translates right away. The free Windows reader runs on your PC (the app tells you if a language is missing in Windows and opens the right settings page); AI reading handles any language but uses many more tokens. Read the notice first: never capture anything private.
- Hear them in my ear: a voice reads the translated captions in your headphones.
- Captions while they talk: words show up while someone is still speaking, then the translation replaces them.
- Answer in their language: when people speak to you in another language, your translations switch to it.
- Languages I speak / around me: "Detect automatically" only picks from these, so short phrases aren't mistaken for another language.
- Cancel before it's spoken: a short wait showing what the voice is about to say, with a Cancel button and key.
- Clean up my swearing: the voice tones down or bleeps your swear words. Captions can hide swearing too (f***).
- Best translations: bigger AI models and more time for the trickiest dialects and slang.
- Try a line: see how Polite, Casual, Very casual and My own say the same thing.
- My word list: names and words that keep coming out wrong, written your way and never translated.
- Captions: sound labels ([laughs], [music]), skip music and noise, big high-contrast text, the same color for the same voice, mute one person (right-click their line), and guessing gender from their words.
- A see-through caption window over the game that clicks pass through (borderless windowed games).
- Quick phrases on the Text page, plus the lines you type most often.
- Learning mode on the Text page: how to read a line (romaji, pinyin...), replay it in its language, and save lines to study.
- Keep a history: the conversation saved to a text file per day, searchable from the app.
- Profiles: save your setup and switch in one click. Share a setup with friends as a code (never your keys, word list or anything you typed).
- More hotkeys: hold for your real voice, and stop speaking.
- Settings > About > Report a problem puts a file on your Desktop with what's needed to find the problem, without keys or anything anyone said.
